📖 Management Summary — Storytelling Checkpoint (Parts 4–7)

1. What is Happening in the Business?

Rift Bloom Roses is a well-established rose export business operating six farms across the Rift Valley. It processes over 2,200 orders per year and generates total revenues of $69.7 million.

While the average order is $31,325, the typical order is worth only $925 — a gap caused by a small number of very large wholesale orders inflating the average. The business is fundamentally healthy, with a customer rating of 4.02/5 and a rejection rate of just 0.18%. However, quality control, payment collection and logistics weaknesses require immediate attention.

2. Where are the Strongest Markets?

Japan leads at $8.3 million, followed by Russia ($6.7M), Norway ($5.5M), Poland ($5.3M) and Switzerland ($5.2M). The top five markets account for 44% of all export revenue.

From a buyer perspective, Interflora Russia is the highest-value client, followed by Tokyo Flower Market, Carrefour Flowers and Marks & Spencer. These four buyers alone contribute over $36 million — more than half of total revenue.

  • Japan, Russia and Norway are the priority markets to protect and grow
  • Australia at $3.7M represents an underdeveloped growth opportunity

3. Which Products are Performing Best?

Explorer leads varieties at $7.3M, followed by Cool Water ($6.7M), Circus ($6.6M) and Sweetness ($5.9M). At the farm level, Ziwa Farm leads with $16.2M followed by Timboroa Farm at $14.9M — together nearly half of all revenue.

  • Explorer, Cool Water and Circus should receive priority production allocation
  • Grade B roses command a HIGHER price than Grade A ($8.30 vs $7.59) — the grading system needs urgent review

4. Where are the Major Quality Problems?

8.8% of orders (196 transactions) recorded a quality issue. The most common problems are Petal Bruising (35), Botrytis (30), Undersized Stems (28), Bud Blast (27) and Wilting (27).

  • Kapseret Farm has the highest rejection rate at 0.27% — immediate quality audit required
  • Shipping temperature does NOT explain rejections (correlation ≈ 0). Problems are at farm level — handling, packaging or harvest timing
  • Cold Chain Break recorded in 20 orders — temperature management during transit needs review

5. What Logistics Problems Exist?

Road then Air shipping is faster (0.7 days vs 3.1 days) and achieves a higher rating (4.08 vs 4.00) but is slightly more expensive ($311 vs $299). The bigger concern is payment collection:

  • Only 38.4% of orders are confirmed paid — a serious cash flow risk
  • 304 orders (13.7%) are overdue — revenue locked up with no recovery timeline
  • 537 orders (24.1%) have an Unknown payment status — these must be investigated immediately

6. What Should Management Do? — Top 5 Recommendations

1

Protect Key Buyers

Interflora Russia, Tokyo Flower Market and Carrefour Flowers generate over $26M combined. Dedicate account managers and guarantee first allocation of premium stock to these three buyers.

2

Expand Top Varieties

Scale production of Explorer, Cool Water, Circus and Sweetness across all farms. These varieties deliver the highest and most consistent revenue returns.

3

Audit Kapseret Farm

With the highest rejection rate (0.27%) and $10.4M in revenue at risk, an immediate quality control review is essential. Root causes must be identified within 60 days.

4

Resolve Overdue Payments

304 overdue orders represent a growing cash flow crisis. Launch a dedicated collections process targeting the oldest invoices first. Investigate 537 orders with Unknown status.

5

Review the Grading System

Grade B roses command higher prices than Grade A — the grading system does not reflect market value. A full pricing and quality audit is needed to realign grades with buyer expectations.
